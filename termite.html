Termite or flying ant?

Both swarm in spring, and they look alike at a glance. Check these three things.

Termite

  • Antennae: straight and bead-like
  • Waist: thick, no pinch
  • Wings: 4, all the same length

Flying ant

  • Antennae: bent at an elbow
  • Waist: pinched and narrow
  • Wings: front pair longer than the back

Not sure what you found? Save one and ask us to identify it.

Winged bugs inside? Do this.

In Hampton Roads, termites swarm from March to May. Winged termites indoors are often the first sign of an infestation.

  1. Don't spray them

    Store bought spray kills the swarmers you see, not the colony that sent them.

  2. Save a few

    Seal some in a bag or jar, or stick them to clear tape, so they can be identified.

  3. Note where

    Window sills, doors and light fixtures near where they appeared are clues.

  4. Call a pro

    We'll confirm what they are and find where they came from.

What's that mud on my foundation?

It may be a mud tube, a termite highway.

Subterranean termites dry out in open air, so they build tunnels of soil to travel from the ground to wood.

Look for

Tunnels about 1/4 to 1/2 inch wide on foundation walls, piers and crawlspace walls. The color can vary with the type of soil.

Don't just scrape it off. Old or empty tubes still matter, and a pro can check whether they're active. Found one? Leave it and call us.

Termite damage or wood rot?

It can be both. Termites like damp wood, and rot can set in after termites damage it.

Termite damage

  • Hollow tunnels that follow the grain
  • Dried mud packed in the tunnels
  • Often hidden behind a solid surface

Wood rot

  • Caused by fungi and moisture
  • Cracks into small cubes or turns soft
  • Not covered by a termite report

Termite FAQ

How do I tell a termite from a flying ant?

Termites have straight, bead-like antennae, a thick waist and four wings of the same length. Flying ants have bent, elbowed antennae, a pinched waist, and front wings longer than the back wings.

What should I do if I find winged bugs inside my house?

Don't spray them, since store bought spray kills the swarmers but not the colony. Save a few in a bag, jar or on clear tape, note where they appeared, and call a pro. In Hampton Roads, termites swarm from March to May, and winged termites indoors are often the first sign of an infestation.

What are mud tubes on my foundation?

Mud tubes are tunnels of soil about 1/4 to 1/2 inch wide that subterranean termites build to travel from the ground to wood without drying out. Look on foundation walls, piers and crawlspace walls. Don't just scrape them off; old or empty tubes still matter, and a pro can check whether they are active.

How can I tell termite damage from wood rot?

Termite damage shows hollow tunnels that follow the grain, packed with dried mud, often hidden behind a solid surface. Wood rot is caused by fungi and moisture and cracks into small cubes or turns soft. It can be both, because termites like damp wood and rot can set in after termite damage. Wood rot is not covered by a termite report.

Are Formosan termites in Hampton Roads?

Yes. Formosan termites were confirmed in a Norfolk building in 2022. They swarm at night on warm evenings in late spring and early summer, fly to lights, form much larger colonies than native termites, and can nest above ground.

What attracts termites to a house?

Common conditions are mulch or soil piled against the foundation, firewood stacked against the house, wood touching the ground, water pooling at the foundation, a damp crawlspace or wood scraps under the house, and branches or shrubs touching the siding.
