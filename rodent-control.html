The Hampton Roads Rodent Problem

Rats and mice are a serious problem for Norfolk, Virginia Beach, and Hampton Roads homeowners, particularly during fall and winter when they seek indoor shelter, and after heavy rains when outdoor habitats flood. Rats contaminate food, damage property, chew electrical wiring, and carry diseases.

Complete Exclusion Approach

Our rodent control programs don't just eliminate existing mouse and rat populations. We identify and seal entry points to prevent future infestations. This comprehensive exclusion approach is essential for Hampton Roads homes, where rodents seek shelter during winter months and after heavy rains.

Signs You May Have Rodents

Hearing scratching sounds in your walls or attic? Finding droppings in your kitchen or pantry? Noticing chewed wires, food packaging, or structural materials? Seeing rodent activity at dusk or dawn? These are all signs it's time to call Pest Heroes.

Where Rodents Get In

Rats and mice can squeeze through openings far smaller than most homeowners expect, which is why a rodent problem in Hampton Roads rarely starts at the front door. It starts at a gap in the foundation, a damaged vent, or an opening around a utility line. Our exclusion process begins by finding every one of those openings and sealing it. Common entry points we address include:

  • Holes and gaps in the foundation, including openings broken through block foundation walls.
  • Foundation vents, especially vents whose mesh screens are torn, chewed, or missing.
  • Gaps around utility lines and old wiring where an exterior opening was never sealed.
  • Openings into attics and crawl spaces, where rodents find the warmth, shelter, and nesting material they are looking for.

Sealing those openings is what separates exclusion from trapping alone. Traps and removal deal with the rodents that are already inside your home. Sealing the access points is what keeps the next ones out. Rodents are looking for the same things people are: warmth, food, and a safe place to nest. A Hampton Roads home offers all three, and that is why rats and mice move inside during fall and winter as the temperatures drop, and again after heavy rains flood their outdoor habitats. Rodents that get into an attic or crawl space also leave droppings and chew through wires and insulation, so catching an entry point early matters. Exclusion also means addressing the conditions that attract rodents in the first place: accessible food sources and nesting materials give rodents a reason to stay, and we look at those during the same visit.
