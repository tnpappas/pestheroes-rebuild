Where Rodents Get In

Rats and mice can squeeze through openings far smaller than most homeowners expect, which is why a rodent problem in Hampton Roads rarely starts at the front door. It starts at a gap in the foundation, a damaged vent, or an opening around a utility line. Our exclusion process begins by finding every one of those openings and sealing it. Common entry points we address include:

  • Holes and gaps in the foundation, including openings broken through block foundation walls.
  • Foundation vents, especially vents whose mesh screens are torn, chewed, or missing.
  • Gaps around utility lines and old wiring where an exterior opening was never sealed.
  • Openings into attics and crawl spaces, where rodents find the warmth, shelter, and nesting material they are looking for.

Sealing those openings is what separates exclusion from trapping alone. Traps and removal deal with the rodents that are already inside your home. Sealing the access points is what keeps the next ones out. Rodents are looking for the same things people are: warmth, food, and a safe place to nest. A Hampton Roads home offers all three, and that is why rats and mice move inside during fall and winter as the temperatures drop, and again after heavy rains flood their outdoor habitats. Rodents that get into an attic or crawl space also leave droppings and chew through wires and insulation, so catching an entry point early matters. Exclusion also means addressing the conditions that attract rodents in the first place: accessible food sources and nesting materials give rodents a reason to stay, and we look at those during the same visit. How do I clean it up?

Don't sweep or vacuum. That can put germs from the droppings into the air. Follow these four steps from the CDC instead.

  1. Air it out

    Open doors and windows for 30 minutes first, and leave the area.

  2. Glove up

    Wear rubber or plastic gloves.

  3. Soak it

    Spray until very wet with disinfectant or 1 part bleach to 9 parts water. Wait 5 minutes.

  4. Wipe and bag

    Use paper towels, bag the trash, then wash your hands.

Rodent Control FAQ

What are the signs of a rodent problem?

Hearing scratching sounds in your walls or attic, finding droppings in your kitchen or pantry, noticing chewed wires, food packaging, or structural materials, and seeing rodent activity at dusk or dawn are all signs it is time to call Pest Heroes.

Are rodents only a winter problem in Hampton Roads?

No. Rats and mice are a serious problem for Norfolk, Virginia Beach, and Hampton Roads homeowners, particularly during fall and winter when they seek indoor shelter, and after heavy rains when outdoor habitats flood. Exclusion and ongoing monitoring are a year-round effort in this region.

How small a gap can a mouse or rat get through?

A mouse can fit through a gap of about 1/4 inch, roughly the width of a pencil. A young rat can fit through about 1/2 inch. Check under roof lines behind gutters, where pipes and wires enter, garage door corners, foundation and dryer vents, and crawlspace doors.

How can I tell mouse droppings from rat droppings?

Mouse droppings are 1/8 to 1/4 inch with pointed ends. Roof rat droppings are about 1/2 inch with pointed ends. Norway rat droppings are about 3/4 inch with blunt ends. In Hampton Roads, our technicians find mostly roof rats and field mice.

How do I clean up rodent droppings safely?

Don't sweep or vacuum, which can put germs into the air. The CDC recommends airing the area out for 30 minutes, wearing rubber or plastic gloves, soaking the droppings with disinfectant or 1 part bleach to 9 parts water for 5 minutes, then wiping them up with paper towels, bagging the trash and washing your hands.
